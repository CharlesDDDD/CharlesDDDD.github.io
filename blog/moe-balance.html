Balance Is a Representation Problem

How well an MoE layer balances its experts follows the shape of its router input.

Setup

One MoE layer. For token $t$:

$$l_t = W x_t, \qquad s_t = \sigma(l_t), \qquad S_t = \operatorname{TopK}_k\big(s_t + b\big),$$

where $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the router input (the output of the pre-MoE RMSNorm; $d = 1024$), $W \in \mathbb{R}^{E\times d}$ is the router weight with one row $w_e \in \mathbb{R}^d$ per expert ($E = 256$), $l_t \in \mathbb{R}^E$ are the logits, $s_t \in (0,1)^E$ the scores ($\sigma$: sigmoid, elementwise), $b \in \mathbb{R}^E$ an expert bias used only to choose experts, and $S_t$ the set of $k = 8$ chosen experts.

$$y_t = \sum_{e\in S_t} g_{t,e}\, f_e(x_t), \qquad g_{t,e} = \frac{s_{t,e}}{\sum_{j\in S_t} s_{t,j}},$$

where $f_e: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is expert $e$ (a SwiGLU MLP of width 256), $g_{t,e}$ its weight, and $y_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ the layer's output.

$$n_e = \big|\{\,t : e \in S_t\,\}\big|, \qquad \bar n = \frac{kN}{E},$$

where $N$ is the number of tokens in one optimizer step ($192 \times 16384 = 3{,}145{,}728$), $n_e$ how many of them go to expert $e$, and $\bar n$ the fair share.

Balanced means $n_e \approx \bar n$ for every expert. Unbalanced experts waste compute (idle experts) and slow down expert-parallel training (busy experts).

Three ways to balance

LBL · auxiliary loss

GShard / Switch; global-batch form as in Qwen
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{bal}} = \alpha E \sum_e \phi_e P_e$$

where $\phi_e = n_e / (kN)$ is expert $e$'s share of slots, $P_e = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t p_{t,e}$ its mean router probability ($p_t \in \mathbb{R}^E$: softmax of $l_t$, or $s_t$ normalized to sum 1 for a sigmoid router), and $\alpha = 8\times10^{-3}$. Counted over the global batch.

acts on: router and its input (gradient)

Bias · sign rule

DeepSeek-V3, aux-loss-free
$$b_e \leftarrow b_e + u\,\operatorname{sign}(\bar n - n_e)$$

after every step, where $u = 10^{-3}$ is a fixed step: under-loaded experts go up, over-loaded go down. No gradient.

acts on: selection only

QB · quantile balancing

Kimi K3
$$b_e \leftarrow q_e - \tfrac{1}{E}\textstyle\sum_j q_j$$

where $q_e$ is the $\bar n$-th smallest $r_{t,e} = \tau_t - s_{t,e}$ over the batch, and $\tau_t$ the $(k{+}1)$-th largest entry of $s_t + b$. $r_{t,e}$ is the bias expert $e$ needs to enter token $t$'s top-$k$, so $b_e = q_e$ would have given expert $e$ exactly $\bar n$ tokens on the last batch (other biases fixed).

acts on: selection only

This post uses the bias rule, the DeepSeek-V3 default.

What we log

Per step, per layer (as in Qwen's ID-balancing paper):

$$\text{MaxVio} = \frac{\max_e n_e - \bar n}{\bar n}, \qquad \text{MinVio} = \frac{\bar n - \min_e n_e}{\bar n}.$$

MaxVio $= 0$: perfect. MaxVio $= E/k - 1 = 31$: one expert is in every token's top-$k$. MinVio $= 1$: some expert got no token. Logged as 10-step means.

For the whole model we report the mean and the worst over the $L = 8$ layers:

$$\text{MaxVio}_{\text{mean}} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \text{MaxVio}_\ell, \qquad \text{MaxVio}_{\text{worst}} = \max_{\ell}\, \text{MaxVio}_\ell,$$

where $\text{MaxVio}_\ell$ is layer $\ell$'s value. For MaxVio$_{\text{worst}}$ we also take the worst single step in each 10-step window; MinVio$_{\text{mean}}$ and MinVio$_{\text{worst}}$ are defined the same way with 10-step means.

Isotropic representation

Over the $N$ tokens of a step:

$$\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t x_t, \qquad \Sigma = \frac{1}{N}\sum_t (x_t-\mu)(x_t-\mu)^\top, \qquad m = \frac{\|\mu\|^2}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_t \|x_t\|^2}, \qquad \text{PR} = \frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^2)},$$

where $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the mean router input, $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ its covariance, $m \in [0,1]$ the mean share, and $\text{PR} \in [1, d]$ the participation ratio.

A representation is isotropic when it has no preferred direction: $\mu = 0$ and $\Sigma \propto I$, i.e. $m = 0$ and $\text{PR} = d$.

  • $m$ is the first-order part. For tokens of equal norm it is the average cosine between two tokens.
  • PR is the second-order part: how many directions the spread really uses.

1Balance follows the representation

Layer 1 has the least isotropic router input: $m = 0.83$, against 0.63 in layer 2 and 0.10 in layer 8. Two random layer-1 tokens have cosine 0.84 (0.003 after removing $\mu$). It is also the only layer that fails to balance (Section 2). Three views link the two.

A toy picture

When every token carries the same large $\mu$, the expert whose weight points along $\mu$ scores highest for every token. Remove $\mu$ and each expert wins its own slice; whiten as well and the slices even out.

Expert by expert

Now the real layer 1 at the end of training. Take the bias away and send each token to its 8 highest logits: which experts win?

Without the bias, the baseline router would starve 103 of 256 experts (under 0.3× their share), and the 8 experts that point most along $\mu$ would take 40% of all slots (fair: 3%). With centering, 47 are starved and those 8 take 2.3%.

Dose by dose

Change only what the layer-1 router reads: raw $x$, then $x - \hat\mu$, then whitened with $a = 1$ and $a = 0.1$. At the end of training its input has 1.4, 88, 777 and 1022 effective directions (participation ratio of $\frac{1}{N}\sum_t z_t z_t^\top$, where $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is what the router reads; for raw $x$ the mean dominates).

The order holds with the bias switched off entirely: experts the router cannot reach on its own (under 0.3× share, end of training) go 103 → 47 → 30 → 15.

Layer 1 is also a token lookup: the token id almost fixes its top-1 expert (normalized mutual information 0.97), and every token's nearest neighbour is another copy of the same word. So the balancer can only move whole words between experts.

2Baseline: bias breaks at layer 1

Run tier_2_80m_lr_1e-2_sf1_e256. 8 layers, $d = 1024$, $E = 256$, $k = 8$; 80M active parameters (1.9B total). Sigmoid router + bias rule ($u = 10^{-3}$), gate weights $g_{t,e}$ not rescaled. MuonH (Muon, with every weight matrix kept at a fixed norm) for matrices, LR $10^{-2}$ (100 warm-up steps, cosine to $10^{-4}$); Adam for embeddings and norms. 5001 steps × 3.1M tokens = 15.7B tokens of FineWeb-Edu. Attention restarts at every document.

Layer 1 starts starving experts near step 700 (Figure 5). From step ~1800, takeovers (one expert in nearly every token's top-8) come and go until step ~3500, each lasting up to ~230 steps. After that MaxVio stays near 7: the busiest expert gets 8× its share.

  • Starvation. At any moment after step 1000, about 26 experts get under a tenth of their share, and about 12 get no token at all.
  • Bias at its limit. Across experts the bias spans about 1.0, the full range of a sigmoid score (about 0.6 with layer-1 centering).
  • Instability. The only run that clips gradients after step 600 (Section 4).

The final loss is not hurt at this scale. But in a run with $g_{t,e}$ ×$\sqrt{8}$ and LR $1.2\times10^{-2}$, the same kind of collapse came with loss spikes to 5.8–7.7.

3Representation centering

Remove the mean before the router reads it:

$$l_t = W\,(x_t - \hat\mu),$$

where $\hat\mu \in \mathbb{R}^d$ estimates $\mu$. One rule: $\hat\mu$ must not see future tokens, so a batch mean is out.

We keep an exponential moving average (EMA) over past steps:

$$\hat\mu \leftarrow \beta_i\, \hat\mu + (1-\beta_i)\, \mu,$$

applied after step $i$, where $\mu$ is that step's token mean and $\beta_i \in [0,1)$ the decay.

It folds into the gate, $l_t = W x_t - W\hat\mu$: one matrix multiply plus one constant per expert. $W$ keeps its exact gradient; $\hat\mu$ is not trained and is saved with checkpoints.

Power EMA

$$\beta_i = \min\!\Big(\beta_{\max},\ \big(1 - \tfrac{1}{i}\big)^{\gamma+1}\Big),$$

where $i$ is the number of updates so far, $\gamma = 17$ and $\beta_{\max} = 0.95$. The EMA spans about the last $i/(\gamma+1)$ updates: short memory early, when the representation moves fastest. The cap ($\approx$20-step window) is reached at update 352. A fixed $\beta$ lags behind in warm-up.

Where

All layers, or layer 1 only.

Differential

$$l_{t,e} = (w_e - \bar w)^\top (x_t - \hat\mu) \;+\; \bar w^\top x_t,$$

where $\bar w = \frac{1}{E}\sum_e w_e \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the average expert's weight. Only the experts' differences are centered; the shared level $\bar w^\top x_t$, which sets where the sigmoid operates, is kept.

Second order: whitening

$$l_t = W\, T\, (x_t - \hat\mu), \qquad T = c\, \max\!\big(\hat\Sigma,\ a\bar\lambda I\big)^{-1/2},$$

where $T \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$, $\hat\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is the EMA covariance (same EMA), $\bar\lambda = \operatorname{tr}\hat\Sigma/d$ its mean eigenvalue, $\max$ acts on eigenvalues, $a > 0$ sets the strength, and the scalar $c$ keeps the total variance, $\operatorname{tr}(T\hat\Sigma T) = \operatorname{tr}\hat\Sigma$. Directions louder than $a\bar\lambda$ come out equally loud; quieter ones keep their relative size. $a \to 0$: full whitening. $a \ge \lambda_{\max}/\bar\lambda$: no change. Folded as $W' = WT$; $T$ is rebuilt every step (one $d\times d$ eigendecomposition).

  • Layer-1 centering: no measurable loss change.
  • Centering every layer costs loss: +0.020 and +0.029 at steps 500 and 1000, so we stopped it. It pins every expert's mean logit at 0, so scores sit near $\sigma(0) = 0.5$; layers 2–8 want low scores and sharp weights. Differential centering keeps the shared level and costs far less: +0.0005 (val), +0.0009 (test).
  • Whitening costs +0.002 at both $a$. Likely reason: MuonH fixes $\|W\|_F$, so the router can grow its logits only by aligning with loud directions, and whitening flattens them (layer-1 logit spread 0.20–0.25 vs 0.39). We have not tested this reason yet.

Late phase: the bias step

In the last ~1000 steps every run, baseline included, gets short spikes (end of Figure 7): a pool of high-bias experts take turns at 2–6× their share for a few steps. At a small LR the router barely moves, so the bias does all the moving, and each $\pm10^{-3}$ step moves whole words between experts.

Test: continue the layer-1-centered run for 200 steps at its final LR, changing only the bias step $u$.

A 10× smaller step cuts layer-1 MaxVio from 2.34 to 0.37 (layer 2: 0.91 → 0.17), and experts above 2× their share go from 3.2 to 0.05 per step. Loss is unchanged (val 2.66449 vs 2.66447). Freezing the bias stops the flips but locks in the last state: one expert stays at about 2.3× (MaxVio 1.31). So the bias step should shrink with the LR, not stop. (DeepSeek-V3 sets it to 0 for its last 500B tokens.)

  • The baseline is the only unstable run. It clips 4 times after step 600, has 5 gradient and 7 loss spike flags of its own, and its gradient norm jumps 11× at step 2600. At step 4400 one batch drives its layer-1 MoE output to 348 and the residual stream to 87 in every layer.

  • Among centered runs the differences are small. Whitening has a little more gradient jitter (0.054 vs 0.046) and heavier logit tails (kurtosis 13 vs 5.4, because its typical logit is smaller); neither shows up in the loss or the gradient norm.

Future work

  • Sparser MoE. More experts at the same $k$ (512, 1024). Layer-1 collapse grows with $E$: on an earlier setup, takeover steps went from 11% at 256 experts to 39% at 512.
  • Bigger models. 260M and 630M active parameters.
  • Change the representation itself. Centering only hides the mean from the router. Next: make layer 1's representation isotropic (mean penalty, dispersive loss) and ask whether the model needs the shared direction at all.
  • Other balancers and seeds. Repeat with LBL and QB; seeds for the small loss gaps; the $\sqrt{8}$ setting that spiked.
