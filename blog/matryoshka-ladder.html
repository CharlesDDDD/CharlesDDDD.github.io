Matryoshka Scaling Ladder

Abstract

A scaling ladder trains one recipe at several sizes and fits a power law to predict larger models. In a standard ladder every rung starts from random weights. In a Matryoshka ladder every rung starts from the trained rung below, grown to the larger size without changing its output. Both ladders train every rung on the same number of tokens, so they cost the same. On mixture-of-experts models from 8 to 14 layers (79M to 414M active parameters), the grown rungs reach a lower loss at every size. Each is worth about 1.25× its compute, so about 20% of the compute is saved at every rung. The Matryoshka ladder follows its own power law, almost parallel to the standard one. Predictions committed before training hit the 14-layer loss of both ladders within 0.05%.

1The standard scaling ladder

A scaling ladder trains the same recipe at a few growing sizes, the rungs. Each rung trains on a token budget proportional to its size (here 100 tokens per parameter), from random weights, with a learning rate and batch size set by rules that scale with the rung. Then one fits a law to the final losses:

$$L(C) = E + A\, C^{-\alpha}, \qquad C = 6ND,$$

where $L$ is the final test loss, $C$ the training compute in FLOPs, $N$ the active non-embedding parameters, $D$ the training tokens, $E$ an irreducible floor, and $A$ and $\alpha$ the scale and slope.

The law is fitted on small rungs and used to predict larger ones. With few rungs the floor $E$ cannot be pinned down, and the usual choice is a pure power law ($E = 0$).

A prediction only counts if it is made before the larger rung is trained. We write the fitting rules down in advance and commit every prediction to git before the rung it predicts starts.

2The Matryoshka ladder

In a standard ladder each rung relearns what the rung below already knows. A Matryoshka ladder reuses it: rung $k{+}1$ starts as the trained rung $k$, grown to the larger shape, and then trains on rung $k{+}1$'s full token budget. Each rung contains the one below, like nested dolls.

Growing a rung

One step adds 2 layers and widens the model (for example 8 → 10 layers, hidden size 1024 → 1280). The grown model computes the same function as the trained small one:

  • New width starts at zero. New hidden dimensions are never written to, and norm gains are rescaled so the old dimensions are normalized as before.
  • New heads and expert neurons read but do not write: their output weights are zero.
  • New layers output zero. They are added after the old ones; their units come in pairs with opposite output weights that cancel. (Plain zero weights do not work, because our optimizer keeps every matrix at a fixed norm.)
  • Old residual branches are rescaled to undo the smaller branch multiplier that deeper models get.

Check, growing a trained 10-layer model to 12 layers: the test loss is 2.55759 before and 2.55761 after growth. The grown model then trains like any run: fresh optimizer state, its own warm-up and decay, and its own LR and batch rule.

Fitting it

The Matryoshka ladder gets its own law, of the same form, fitted to the grown rungs only (the smallest rung is shared by both ladders and is a standard run). It does not have to follow the standard law. What matters is that it predicts its own larger rungs.

Measuring the gain

At rung $k$, with final losses $L^{\text{std}}_k$ (standard) and $L^{\text{M}}_k$ (Matryoshka):

  • Gain $G_k = L^{\text{std}}_k - L^{\text{M}}_k$.
  • Compute multiplier $M_k$: the standard ladder would need $M_k$ times rung $k$'s compute to reach $L^{\text{M}}_k$, read off the standard law. The Matryoshka rung saves a share $1 - 1/M_k$ of the compute.

Both ladders train the same rungs on the same tokens, so they cost the same; any gain is free. For a fair comparison each ladder gets its own tuned LR and batch rules (Section 3).

3Experimental setup

Model. A mixture-of-experts decoder. Every layer has attention and an MoE block with 128 experts, 8 active per token. Hidden size is $128 \times$ layers. Attention uses a 512-token sliding window, with a global layer every fourth layer. Experts are balanced with Quantile Balancing (arXiv 2607.24653).

Data. FineWeb-Edu, Llama-3 tokenizer, sequences of 16,384 tokens. Every run is scored on the same held-out test set of 63M tokens.

Optimizer. MuonH and AdamH (Muon and Adam with every weight matrix kept at a fixed norm, no weight decay). The LR transfers across depth and tokens with HyperP (arXiv 2603.28743); warm-up of 100 steps, then cosine decay.

Rungs. 8 to 18 layers, 100 tokens per active parameter:

Batch in sequences of 16,384 tokens. The Matryoshka ladder uses larger batches, and so fewer steps for the same tokens. 8–12L have two seeds per ladder, which gives the seed noise; larger rungs have one.

Tuning both ladders. A grown model starts partly trained, so it may want a different LR and batch than a fresh one. We tuned each ladder the same way: a grid of LR and batch at 10 layers, then rules that carry the best values to other sizes (details in the appendix). The two ladders end up with different rules:

  • The Matryoshka ladder wants a lower LR (0.70× the base at batch 72, vs 1.09×) and is insensitive to batch size.
  • The standard ladder prefers small batches, so it uses about 0.6× the Matryoshka batch.

At 12 layers, 0.71× and 1.41× the rule's LR are both worse than the rule in both ladders, so the rules hold at a larger size.

Fitting rules, fixed before the runs they score: seeds are averaged; each ladder is fitted on its own rungs; with fewer than 5 rungs the main fit is a pure power law; a prediction passes if it is within $2\sqrt{\text{SE}^2 + \sigma^2}$ of the result, where SE is the fit's standard error and $\sigma = 0.0019$ the seed noise of one run.

4.3 Where the saving comes from

A grown rung starts from the rung below, so a natural guess is that the saving is the compute it reuses. Write the compute a grown rung is worth as its own plus a fraction $\kappa$ of its donor's:

$$M_k\, C_k = C_k + \kappa_k\, C_{k-1}.$$

$C_k$: compute of rung $k$. $\kappa = 1$: the donor's compute carries over in full.

  • Only the last donor counts. A 12L grown from the standard 10L ends at the same loss as the Matryoshka 12L (2.3935 vs 2.3934), although its donor is 0.032 worse. What matters is the compute of the rung it grows from, not the whole chain.

5.3 Loss collapses and load balancing

With sign-rule balancing, standard runs at 10 layers and deeper sometimes collapsed: the loss jumped by more than 1 nat, then came back. Grown runs never did. That raised a question: was the gain growth, or just avoiding collapses?

6Limits and next steps

  • Few rungs. 4 standard and 3 Matryoshka rungs so far, so the floor $E$ is not identified, and one prediction has been scored.
  • Seeds. Two per ladder at 8–12L; one from 14L on.
  • Small models. Downstream accuracy is near chance on some tasks.
  • Next. 16L and 18L for both ladders, scored against the predictions above; whether $\kappa$ keeps falling, and what sets it.

A.1 Tuning grid

At 10 layers we trained each ladder at batch 36, 72 and 144 with 2–4 learning rates each (7 standard runs, 8 Matryoshka runs, all grown from the same 8L), and fitted a quadratic surface in log LR and log batch to the final loss. The best LR at each batch gives the LR rule

$$\eta_{\text{ref}} = 0.012 \cdot m \left(\frac{B}{72}\right)^{q},$$

where $B$ is the batch in sequences and $m$, $q$ come from the table (standard $m = 1.09$, $q = 0.48$; Matryoshka $m = 0.70$, $q = 0.38$). The grid used sign-rule balancing; we kept the rules when switching to Quantile Balancing.
